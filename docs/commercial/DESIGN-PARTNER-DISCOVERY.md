# MOSAIC Design-Partner Discovery — Supplier & Operations Management

**Purpose:** Validate the first commercial wedge without weakening the platform-first strategy. This is discovery, not a sales promise or production pilot.

## Target participants

Interview 10–15 people across South African SMEs and mid-market organisations:
- Head of Operations / COO
- Procurement or supplier-management lead
- Finance operations lead
- Compliance / risk operations lead
- IT or business systems owner

Avoid relying only on friends or people in one industry. Seek organisations with meaningful supplier/customer onboarding volume and fragmented workflows.

## Interview guide (30–40 minutes)

1. Walk me through how a new supplier is onboarded from request to first payment.
2. Which teams touch the process, and where are hand-offs made?
3. Which systems, spreadsheets, inboxes and external providers are involved?
4. Where do delays, rework, errors or control failures occur?
5. How do you verify company registration, tax, banking and supporting documents?
6. How are approvals, exceptions, evidence and audit trails handled?
7. What happens when a supplier's information changes or a review expires?
8. What does this process cost in staff time, external fees and delayed operations?
9. What have you already tried? Why did it fail or remain incomplete?
10. What security, privacy, data-residency, procurement and integration requirements would a new platform need to satisfy?
11. Who owns the budget and who approves a new operational platform?
12. If this problem were solved, what measurable result would justify paying for it?

Do not lead with the MOSAIC feature list. Ask for recent examples and quantify frequency, impact and current workaround.

## Hypothesis to test

Organisations want to configure and own operational applications and processes without buying a rigid suite or funding a bespoke software project for every workflow. Supplier onboarding is a concrete entry workflow that can prove configurable forms, data, approvals, integrations, AI-assisted document handling and auditability.

## Evidence to capture

For each interview record:
- Organisation size and industry (with permission)
- Process volume and cycle time
- Number of hand-offs and systems
- Error/rework rate and measurable impact
- Current software and workaround
- Security/data constraints
- Buyer, budget owner and procurement path
- Must-have integrations
- Willingness to participate in design sessions
- Whether a funded pilot is plausible and what gates it requires

Do not collect personal, confidential, banking or supplier documents during discovery.

## Go / no-go signals

Continue investing in the supplier-management wedge if:
- At least 6 of 10 qualified interviews report a recurring, material problem.
- At least 3 organisations agree to a structured design-partner workshop.
- At least 1 buyer identifies a credible budget and procurement path for a paid pilot, conditional on security approval.

Re-scope if pain is weak, buyers already have satisfactory solutions, integration costs dominate value, or security/procurement barriers make the wedge impractical.

## Design-partner offer

Offer a co-design programme, not production access:
- Map one workflow and its measurable baseline.
- Co-design a configurable solution.
- Demonstrate only with synthetic or explicitly approved non-sensitive data.
- Agree success metrics, security gates, responsibilities and exit criteria in writing.
- Do not promise production deployment, certifications, integrations or timelines before technical validation.

## Founder safeguards

Build independently. Do not use an employer's confidential information, code, systems, customer data, vendor contracts or internal architecture. Do not imply employer endorsement. Review employment, IP assignment, conflict-of-interest and outside-business policies before commercial engagement.
